These are proposed directions after the current continuation, not an extension of its frozen protocol. No current-run outcomes were inspected for this note, and no simulations, builds, random draws, production edits or new ABI changes were made. The objective is a single unchanged genome that can acquire useful information and reuse behavior across unfamiliar situations, eventually competing with strong practical handwritten programs across a stated range of problems.

My preferred direction is a small world of **manipulable resource connections**. Its actions change which future opportunities exist, making it a substantial departure from pursuing a supplied target or copying a supplied sample. If the current continuation produces mostly pursuit or economy gains without useful cue dependence, I would instead favor the simpler **persistent resource landscape** below: it offers frequent, observable consequences throughout development without another long forced blank. These are choices to reconsider from the complete result, not success gates or an automatic sequence. Perfect old-task retention and a hand-built winning tissue are unnecessary.

The evidence supports this change in emphasis. AO added acquisition late in one branch, but its gains did not establish dark-period memory; changing allocation traded retention against other captures. The six-genome matching baseline inherited competent pursuit with almost universal broad-shape preference. The information instrument establishes a retained-cue payoff of 2.945493 ±0.143336 F, but its computation is external and its finite reflex controls made no choices. We should not make reproducing that controller the research objective. Independently renewing food/harm targets and equal food twins were already explored; motor-law switching also has older external-controller evidence. None should be presented as a newly discovered interaction structure. All three candidates preserve the actual cell interface: a sense-plane cell receives only delayed receptor events generated at its own screen location; act-plane twitches move the body and the hotspot clicks. Cells receive no object ID, map, reward, inventory bit, global clock, episode phase or task label. World dynamics may have their own event times, just as targets currently do. Local sensory fields can depict objects and their physical consequences through the existing scalar modality. Crucially, **a change in the fitness ledger is not feedback the organism can learn from**. Any proposed learning from consequences must have a corresponding localized physical observation. | Candidate | What an action changes | Distinct capability to investigate |
|---|---|---|
| Persistent resource landscape | Local stock and the value of later revisits | Allocation among changing opportunities; learning useful properties of places |
| Probeable objects | The observations available before consumption | Deciding whether and where information is worth obtaining |
| Manipulable resource connections | The causal route by which resources become available elsewhere | Reusing action consequences across layouts and compositions |

**1. Persistent resource landscape: harvest changes the future.** Put three persistent patches at world-fixed positions. Each has bounded stock and a fixed replenishment law drawn at life start, independent of its visible shape, location and initial stock. A harvest click removes an available packet; empty attempts cannot create food. Stock replenishes locally, without replacing the patch around the organism. Its ordinary local field changes with stock and harvest, so consumption and recovery have observable consequences. Low-level persistent landmarks can distinguish places through spatial observation; their appearance must not encode productivity. Physical parameters and distributions stay fixed throughout the evolutionary run.

This differs from independent target renewal: the organism can exhaust its own opportunities, leave them, and return to consequences of its earlier actions. A good immediate target can be a poor long-run use of time. The same genome might learn which places recover quickly in each life, or develop a simpler successful patrol. We should accept either as behavior and distinguish them empirically, rather than reward a named memory implementation. Continuous stock cues may let a reactive harvester succeed; that is a legitimate alternative, not something to hide after the fact.

The smallest useful pilot is three patches, two replenishment laws and one fixed distribution of layouts, over a life long enough for several harvest/revisit cycles after one-cell development. A proposed T512 is a starting design value, not measured accessibility. Counterbalance shape/productivity bindings. Compare an unchanged endpoint against its ancestor, greedy visible-stock pursuit, staying at one patch, and a tuned fixed patrol. A transfer test must reserve a new assignment/layout combination; later claims of structural reuse would need changed patch counts and replenishment combinations as well. Report resource obtained, empty visits, travel, time allocation and all costs. An early-to-late improvement alone is not learning evidence: depletion, maturation and geometry also change over a life.

**2. Probeable objects: observation becomes a choice.** Give an object a shell that responds locally when touched by a click in its outer region; clicking its core consumes it. The shell's response is a transient spatial field whose form gives noisy evidence about the object's contents. Untested objects have matched appearance regardless of contents. The organism can inspect, consume directly, inspect another object, or depart. Neither probing nor waiting earns reward. A probe consumes time and ordinary physical activity; it may also alter a finite object resource if that is part of the explicit physical model. Its response reaches cells only through their local receptors.

The change from delayed matching is causal: useful evidence is obtained by an action the organism chooses, rather than delivered on a global sample/blank/choice schedule. There need be no enforced blank or prescribed probe-then-consume sequence. Include fixed cases where inspection is informative and cases where direct consumption is a reasonable alternative, so “always do the extra click” is not automatically the intended solution. The first small pilot could use two objects and two shell-response reliabilities, with independently randomized geometry and contents; proposed T256 allows repeated encounters rather than one cue during early growth.

Measure the net value of inspection and subsequent conditional consumption, including ignored objects and failed probes. Strong comparisons include never-probe, always-probe and a tuned finite evidence accumulator using the same observations/actions. One unchanged genome should transfer across a reserved combination of response reliability, object layout and contents frequency. That would establish limited active-information reuse, not general reasoning. This is the closest candidate to matching and my second choice: it could otherwise become another specialized two-class detector. No shell response may be an uncharged privileged correctness bit.

**3. Manipulable resource connections: change the world before collecting.** A small visible source feeds finite outlet reservoirs through local diverter valves. A click at a valve changes its physical routing state; a click at a reservoir harvests available stock. Valve orientation, local flow and reservoir filling are represented by spatial fields. Flow has finite transit time along fixed connections. Switching earns no income; only harvested resource does. A diverter conserves source flow, so opening everything cannot multiply reward. Some flow can return to the source rather than reach a harvestable outlet, without creating an arbitrary wrong-answer penalty.

Start with one source, two valves and two outlets, with counterbalanced connection/layout variants. Objects stay at world-fixed positions. The organism can discover useful action effects by acting and revisiting them, follow visible connections, exploit a simple repeated route, or find another strategy. No list of required stages is exposed or rewarded. It is a causal resource system, not a rule that credits “first click A, then click B.” Existing movement and clicking are sufficient as actions; the added work is world object state, local field rendering and accounting across C/Python/JS, not a new organism channel. Proposed T512 would give several opportunities after development; the travel and fill times need a concrete bounded contract before execution.

The smallest informative pilot uses only the two-valve family. Freeze one endpoint across a reserved connection/layout combination and compare it with its ancestor plus tuned greedy, exhaustive valve-toggle and small map/state-controller baselines. Report harvested output, valve actions, wasted flow, travel and cost, along with performance conditional on the actual connection structure. A simple toggle-and-follow policy may solve this first family; that is useful accessibility evidence, not evidence of advanced planning. The stronger eventual claim would require the same genome handling unseen combinations or longer compositions of the learned physical relations. Merely translating or rotating familiar layouts is weaker. Persistent valve state can also serve as external memory, so successful routing alone would not establish cellular memory.

For whichever candidate is selected, the smallest evolutionary study I would consider informative is one ordinary eight-generation continuation of the complete ordered population, on one fixed distribution of that world's small instances. With the current 256 candidates, eight initial lives and eight extra lives for the top 64, that is **20,480 selection calls**. A fixed ancestor and fixed final endpoint on three declared development/transfer contexts at 512 shared fresh lives add **3,072 confirmation calls**. Native diagnostics, startup and engineering calls need their own exact inventory before any launch; 23,552 is only the sum of those two scientific components. Eight generations can reveal an accessible response but cannot establish a ceiling after AO's observed late improvement. This is a bounded proposal, not a new execution request, CPU commitment or automatic follow-up. Earlier skills can be measured alongside it without requiring perfect retention or starting another allocation search.

No small candidate by itself fulfills the user's broader objective. A meaningful claim beyond practical handwritten solutions needs a declared range of contexts, substantial and frozen handwritten competitors, and reserved cases unavailable to either their tuning or endpoint selection. Compare breadth and adaptation of one unchanged genome, rather than choosing a different champion for each case. Baselines must actually execute competent physical actions; the current zero-choice reflex controls are insufficient for that claim. Keep sensory/action access comparable and disclose external compute separately from cellular physical cost. A fixed genome improving its decisions from new local evidence within a life is a different result from additional evolution, fresh random seeds, or retaining a familiar reflex. Wider composition and independent evolutionary replication would strengthen the eventual claim, but neither is a prerequisite to trying one concrete new interaction now.
