A limited cost calculation from the completed external information experiment

This is post hoc arithmetic on saved results, not another experiment or an argument that a particular local
memory circuit exists. Retaining the sample was worth 2.945493 ±0.143336 F. At the unchanged
maintenance price of 0.02 energy per cell per time unit, that difference is equivalent to maintaining
147.27 additional cells throughout the T85 life, before any division, firing, communication or
movement costs. The lower-three-SE difference is equivalent to 125.77 such cells.

This helps put the incentive scale in perspective: the measured reward for using earlier information is
substantial compared with the maintenance of a few additional cells. It does not measure the real local
implementation cost, prove evolutionary accessibility, identify an optimal penalty, or show that more cells
would help. The instrument gets central computation for free and starts fully assembled; an evolved body
must develop and implement its own computation. Different actions, sensing or body geometry could change
both benefits and costs. Keep physical prices fixed for the current continuation and examine its complete
behavior/cost result before deciding whether an incentive change is the highest-value next experiment.
