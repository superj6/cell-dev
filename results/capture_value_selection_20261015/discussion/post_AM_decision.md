# After the complete AM result: one longer continuation with longer cue gaps

**Recommend one eight-generation ordinary continuation from all 32 AM current-value survivors, at capture value 500, adding a fixed persistent-target context with mean dark intervals of 20 instead of 5.** Keep earlier tasks in selection and measure the unchanged midpoint and final genomes across contexts. This combines the demonstrated ability to accumulate capture gains with a nearby information challenge, without spending another experiment on prices or task weights.

The complete result changes the earlier option sketch. Current-value evolution improved captures over its AL start in all four measured contexts: POOL **+1.332 ± 0.229**, persistent **+0.865 ± 0.135**, brief blink **+0.443 ± 0.129**, and turn-plus-blink **+0.695 ± 0.128**. Both old-task retention margins pass. The extra ordinary costs are about 0.15–0.18 F, with roughly six or seven additional cells. Thus neither a task-weight repair nor a computational-cost discount is presently required to obtain useful acquisition. Higher capture value gives another **+1.568 ± 0.242** POOL captures relative to current value, but its persistent difference is **−0.434 ± 0.151**, unresolved at the fixed three-SE criterion; it has no joint price advantage and substantially higher body/miss costs. Keep 500 rather than promoting 2,000 as the default.

There is a concrete next weakness. Current-value captures fall from **5.324 persistent to 4.535 in 80-on/5-off blink**. Its own-start capture gain is **0.421875 ± 0.132148 smaller in blink than in steady persistent**, a negative interaction beyond three SE. Its dark-capture gain is unresolved (**−0.0293 ± 0.0198**). This does not prove missing memory, but it makes resilience to longer cue gaps a more informative target than another visible-target-only continuation. Changing `visibility_off` from 5 to 20 preserves target motion, signal geometry and conduction, unlike jumping immediately to the older dark-flash task. It also changes visible-time fraction and encounter history, so any benefit would not isolate memory.

Proposed fixed scope:

- Import the entire ordered current-value final population; start fresh native/genetic streams, with no founder expansion or resumed state. Use eight generations, ordinary 32+224, 8 initial plus 8 recheck lives, no structural mutations and all current physical prices unchanged.
- In every eight-life selection block, use two POOL, two steady persistent and four persistent **80-on/20-off, low-zero** lives. Repeat that fixed allocation in the recheck suffix. Use ABI4 with neutral visibility for the old contexts. The environment never responds to population performance.
- Preserve generation-four and generation-eight rank-zero checkpoints before any confirmation. Confirm the start, fixed midpoint and fixed final endpoint on five worlds: POOL, steady persistent, existing 80/5 blink, new 80/20 blink, and its predeclared `tau_turn=100` combination. Use 256 fresh common seeds per world. Verify prior exposure before labeling the last combination held out; all previously observed contexts remain development evidence. Never substitute the better midpoint for the declared final endpoint.
- The primary acquisition contrast is final-minus-start captures in 80/20 blink, accompanied by direct 90% capture-retention margins in POOL and steady persistent. Report midpoint-to-final change and the paired gain interaction between 80/20 and 80/5 as secondary evidence. Use the same descriptive three-SE convention, include unfavorable outcomes and all guards/extinctions, and retain costs, misses/accuracy and dark captures. A positive result establishes a local capability gain with measured retention, not a memory algorithm or reliable curriculum.

One eight-generation search uses **20,534 research lives**: 20,480 selection, 48 periodic validation at generations 0/4/7, and six tiny final diagnostics. Confirmation adds **3,840**, with six existing startup calls if the AM identity/ABI4 checks are reused: **24,380 total calls**. No extra evolution is needed to preserve the midpoint. At the older conservative rate, search projects **6,468 CPU seconds** and confirmation **1,356**, including the existing 1.5 multiplier and 180 seconds overhead per phase. All 16 AM row rates are below that floor. A proposed **9,000 CPU-second ceiling, 7,200 search / 1,800 confirmation**, one two-thread worker and unchanged memory safeguards is plausible; new bodies can still exceed historical costs, so preserve hard-stop/no-rescue rules.

Why this rather than the alternatives: eight generations give useful variants more opportunity to combine than another four-generation perturbation, at almost the same search-call budget as two four-generation arms. A single branch cannot causally attribute gains to the new allocation or establish that eight generations are generally better; the question is whether this continuing lineage can acquire and retain capability. Another matched pair is preferable only if the immediate goal is a policy-effect estimate. Circuit probes explain an existing gain but do not add capability; they can follow a substantial, reproducible cue-gap gain. Task reweighting and gene duplication remain available hypotheses, but AM does not demonstrate either bottleneck. This is one proposed experiment, not a predetermined curriculum or implementation authorization.
