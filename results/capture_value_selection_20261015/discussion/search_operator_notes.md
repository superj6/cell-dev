# Search operators and retention of interacting genes

The overlooked candidate bottleneck is **inheritance of a useful combination of genes**, rather than a demonstrated limit on what one cell can compute. Uniform crossover keeps each individual gene together, but it does not keep a multi-gene regulatory circuit together. This is a source-backed mechanism worth remembering; existing experiments have not established that it is the dominant search bottleneck.

**What the code guarantees.** In `src/genome.py:245`, a life crossover unit contains gene k's incoming W row, private parameters, active bit and A readout column. However, its outgoing regulatory coefficient W[j,k] belongs to recipient gene j's unit. Independent fair parent choices across units (`src/genome.py:132`, `src/genome.py:402`) can therefore combine one parent's gene state/response with the other parent's downstream coefficients. A gene-origin ID does not identify the ancestry of its entire outgoing circuit. As a conditional illustration, if two parents differ at four interacting gene units, the probability of inheriting all four from either one parent is 1/8 before mutation. This is arithmetic about unit masks, **not** an estimated probability of preserving behavior: related parents may share values, mixed combinations can work or improve, and the separate global unit also matters.

Every fresh offspring then receives point mutations; there is no designated unmutated offspring stream (`src/evolve.py:543`). With eight active genes and the current settings, there are two distinct mutated entries, both in one randomly chosen block: regulation or phenotype/readout (`src/genome.py:410`, `src/genome.py:428`). A particular repair requiring one regulatory and one readout change cannot be supplied by that single mutation step; it needs sequential inheritance or recombination. This makes coordinated improvements a plausible accessibility problem. It is not proof of a fitness valley, nor evidence that increasing mutation count would help. The 32 old parents also enter the 256-candidate evaluation unchanged, so crossover does **not** mechanically destroy every working parent; loss requires subsequent selection. All parents are reevaluated, and neither a founder nor an earlier champion has permanent protection.

**What completed D/G/M establish.** Ordinary D's two 16-generation continuations improved fresh F by +0.633±0.188 and +0.756±0.202, with capture-funded gains. The same operators can accumulate useful changes. G's two matched structural searches had unresolved advantages over D (−0.252±0.209 and +0.325±0.193 F); this comparison changed duplication/deletion and gene-count-scaled mutation together, not crossover. J/M found no final copied-gene contribution in their registered conditions. M nevertheless found a large whole-life source-gene effect (+7.277±0.129 F lost on deletion), which can include development; this is not a circuit-computation measurement.

The completed G connectivity audit is particularly relevant: 4/11 copied origins appeared in offspring with coefficient paths to readouts, and 2/7 in retained carriers. The eventually disconnected G51089 origin had twelve earlier connected offspring observations, one retained. Thus useful-looking connections can appear and disappear during search, but coefficient connectivity is not demonstrated functional value. The audit explicitly allows crossover to erase or combine paths; it does not attribute each disappearance to crossover or establish that selection lost a beneficial circuit. Silent duplicates are created **after** that offspring's point mutations, so their new outgoing effects also require a later step. These facts make the final copy nulls poor evidence for a capacity ceiling.

**How much weight to put on this.** There is no matched crossover-versus-clonal comparison for these present ABI3/4 life lineages. Older, different-substrate context/jump experiments favored crossover (RESULTS 2026-10-06); they caution against assuming recombination is generally harmful, but do not settle the current question. Price incentives, task information demand, noisy ranking and short searches remain alternative explanations. The current structural-off runs retain the existing eight active slots rather than testing recruitment of new ones; unused slots alone do not imply inadequate capacity.

Keep the next priority on the proposed environment edge where information-dependent behavior pays. This note does not propose changing the current run, reserve a namespace, or justify a new operator study now. If a later study isolates heritable multi-gene dependence but repeatedly loses it despite an appropriate objective, preservation of linked combinations would then be a concrete search hypothesis to test—not a reason to hand-design those combinations.
