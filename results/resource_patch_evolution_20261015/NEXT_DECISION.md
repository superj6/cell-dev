# Next investment: preserve useful partial acquisition during selection

The completed pilot improves economy without a resolved patch-acquisition gain, while POOL and reserved
transfer acquisition decline. Do not simply extend this exact objective or replace its final endpoint.
The next bounded comparison will test ordinary mean-F selection against preserving nondominated candidates
on three task-specific capture means plus ordinary aggregate F. A candidate can therefore survive because
it offers useful acquisition somewhere, even when a single weighted average would discard it. Ordinary
crossover may later combine useful variants; that is a hypothesis, not a demonstrated mechanism.

Keep the full final population as the starting material, fixed worlds/exposure/prices and ordinary mutation
operators. Apply the comparison to both the first shortlist and the combined recheck results. Select one
final reporting genome from each branch by the same predeclared rule favoring joint acquisition, using only
selection data. Evaluate those unchanged genomes on earlier tasks and a new reserved resource combination.
A portfolio of specialists is not success on the user's generalization goal. Costs remain visible because
acquisition-prioritized selection can produce expensive bodies.

A short four-generation matched comparison is the leading design; source review, exact call/budget counts,
new seed audit and prospective publication remain before any execution. The current study is closed and
this note releases no new life. No new organism input, clock, hand-designed behavior, population-responsive
world or paid resource is involved.

This chooses the partial-variant approach over the independently suggested hard acquisition-retention
constraint. Requiring every candidate to retain every ability could discard useful intermediate variants.
The tradeoff is that nondominated selection can preserve noisy extremes or specialists. The common fixed
endpoint rule and fresh whole-genome evaluation must expose that failure mode. This is one focused search
comparison, not another broad allocation or price sweep and not an assumption that more depth cannot help.
