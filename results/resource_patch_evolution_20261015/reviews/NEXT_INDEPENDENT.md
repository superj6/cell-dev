# Independent recommendation after the resource-patch continuation

A focused selection-objective comparison has the highest expected value next. Keep the current physical
worlds, prices and local asynchronous cells. Test whether selection can add acquisition while protecting
inherited acquisition, before spending another run mainly changing exposure or letting the same scalar
objective operate longer. This is a recommendation from one completed continuation, not a registered run.

The final patch gain is **0.326172 ±0.328844 captures** and **0.373047 ±0.302795 refill packets**, both
unresolved. Production F rises **0.595798 ±0.161773**, with **0.436534 F** supplied by lower physical costs:
about 73% of the mean gain. The same endpoint loses **1.861328 ±0.216181 POOL captures** and
**1.382812 ±0.267868 transfer captures**, including **0.929688 ±0.216024 transfer refill packets**.
Persistent and longer-gap F increase despite negative capture point estimates. All uncertainties here are
paired SE over 512 life clusters, conditional on these fixed genomes.

Those observations expose two distinct issues. Aggregate net F can improve through economy while
acquisition is unchanged or worse; weighted mixtures can trade an older ability against benefits elsewhere.
The confirmation-weighted 50/25/25 F gain has a positive point estimate, about 0.216, even with the POOL loss.
That arithmetic does not prove why individual mutations survived or show that noisy selection is harmless.
All six reported 90%-retention margins remain unresolved at three SE. The POOL point estimate is below 90%,
but this is **not** evidence establishing loss greater than 10%.

My preferred next comparison is the existing mean-F selection against one **fixed-reference acquisition
retention rule**, with acquisition progress given explicit priority among acceptable candidates and original
price F retained as an efficiency criterion. A concrete candidate is: retain POOL and persistent capture
rates relative to the fixed AO reference; rank feasible candidates by patch acquisition, using original F to
resolve equal acquisition scores; rank infeasible candidates by their worst normalized retention deficit.
The reference, normalization and tolerance must remain fixed for the whole search. Any allowed loss, such as
the existing 10% convention, must be stated as a new selection choice rather than silently promoted from a
descriptive diagnostic. More stringent lossless retention may stall an otherwise useful path.

This deliberately changes reproductive selection, not food physics or organism inputs. It is warranted to
test the user's acquisition/retention goal directly, but it can produce expensive bodies. Report all original
price F and energy costs, and judge a capture gain with its cost rather than declaring every gain useful.
Constrain **captures**, not just F: an F-retention constraint or worst normalized F objective still permits
the persistent-world pattern already observed. Conversely, a retention constraint followed only by mean F
can preserve older captures yet continue selecting patch economy with no acquisition gain. Worst normalized
capture progress across all three training worlds is a reasonable alternative, but it introduces a noisier
bottleneck objective; I would not add both alternatives as another broad panel.

Use two contemporaneous arms from the same complete ordered AO population, identical fixed exposures,
mutation rules, generation budget and predetermined endpoints. Starting again from AO is a new controlled
comparison, not replacement of this study's generation-eight result by its midpoint. Use matched evaluation
draws and account explicitly for any reference evaluations. Two lives per old world are a noisy basis for
a hard constraint: freeze a recheck/aggregation rule for both arms, without outcome-triggered resampling or
three-SE admission tests on such tiny batches. These details need a concrete protocol before execution.
Current confirmation cases can inform that design but become development evidence for the new study.
Keep new confirmation seeds independent; the now-observed transfer combination is no longer a novel reserved
case. Test both fixed descendants on all retained worlds and one newly reserved combination without choosing
an endpoint for each environment. One matched pair still cannot estimate reliability across searches.

The competing directions remain useful, but currently rank lower:

| Direction | What it would resolve | Why it is secondary now |
| --- | --- | --- |
| More generations | Whether later search finds acquisition paths | AO previously benefited from depth, so eight generations establish no ceiling. The present objective still rewards observed tradeoffs. |
| More old-world exposure | Whether extra weight or precision reduces loss | The prior AO allocation comparison already found a retention/acquisition tradeoff; another ratio also mixes weighting with precision. |
| Another price comparison | Whether added acquisition justifies greater energy use | The AM reward comparison did not establish a joint advantage from quadrupled food value. Directly testing retention and acquisition targets the present mismatch more cleanly. |
| Circuit or stock-cue intervention | Whether new behavior uses depletion information differently | Valuable after a functional acquisition/transfer gain. Existing ancestors already benefit from stock cues; this endpoint does not yet show added patch acquisition. |

No new controller project, substrate change, memory requirement or hand-built-body success gate is needed
for that comparison. Failure would also be informative: retained acquisition may be unavailable in the local
mutation neighborhood, unaffordable, or obscured by scoring noise. It would not establish a general capacity
ceiling. Success would establish a better observed tradeoff, with mechanism and broad generality still open.
