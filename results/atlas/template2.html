Spiking Tissue Atlas

Every body below grew from one cell. A genome decides how its cells divide and what each one becomes, and a grown cell only ever hears its immediate neighbours. Nobody wired an input or an output.

Part 1 is the newest work: bodies that chase a target across a screen during one continuous life, with no clock anywhere. Part 2 is the first task ladder, where flat bodies answered short timed trials. Pick a body: the replay starts mid-life, and “From the first cell” replays its growth from the zygote.

Bodies that steer a dot

The body is a dot on a screen, and its cells are stacked in layers. The bottom layer touches the screen: a cell there gets a receptor event now and then, more often when the target's halo is under it. A spike in the layer above twitches the dot outward on that cell's side. The middle cell of that layer clicks, and a click with the target inside the catch zone is a catch. Higher layers can only talk to the cells below them. Each cell fires when its own charge crosses threshold, and a spike takes about one time unit to reach a neighbour.

Score is catch income minus the cost of living cells, spikes and wasted clicks, per time unit. The replays below are one typical life of each body (the life whose score is nearest the median of 24), replayed in the reference simulator and checked against the fast one bit for bit.

Flat bodies answering timed trials

An earlier model on a flat grid with a global tick. Each organism grew, then faced short trials: grown cells react to light where they sit and pass spikes to their neighbours, and the score depends only on where in the body the firing ends up.
