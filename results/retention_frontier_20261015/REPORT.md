# AK result: persistent-target progress transfers nearby, but ancestral ability is lost

The latest genome catches 38.6% fewer targets in ancestral POOL than its older L92 ancestor. Its persistent-target capture improvement repeats on fresh lives and transfers to two nearby environments. This is task-specific progress with a substantial old-task trade-off, not cumulative retention or a newly identified algorithm.

All three saved genomes were fixed before the experiment. Each received the same 256 fresh seeds in each of four worlds, with unchanged prices, local asynchronous rules and ordinary development.

The old-task loss is already visible in the first mutation: AE minus L92 loses 1.464844 ± 0.348044 POOL captures. The later AI step loses another 5.863281 ± 0.323560. AI retains 61.407% of L92's mean POOL captures. The predeclared conventional 90%-retention diagnostic fails clearly: the paired AI−0.9×L92 vector is −5.429297 ± 0.328948, with upper three-SE bound −4.442452. Persistent retention relative to AE passes, with actual capture improvement.

Cheaper bodies accompany both the gains and losses. Mean terminal cells in POOL fall 116.133 → 97.961 → 58.770 across the lineage, and in persistent they fall 129.586 → 108.414 → 64.270. Cost saving explains about 77% of AI's persistent fitness gain over AE. In POOL the capture-income loss exceeds the savings, so fitness also falls. These observations do not isolate body size as the causal mechanism; each comparison changes the whole genotype and permits development.

The AI-minus-AE capture advantage is larger by an unresolved 0.066406 ± 0.226664 under faster turns and 0.207031 ± 0.165222 under darkness than in persistent. Thus the data show nearby transfer, without demonstrating improved perturbation robustness. AI's own change from persistent is +0.242188 ± 0.164895 captures under faster turns and −0.312500 ± 0.118262 under darkness. Neither passes the descriptive three-SE threshold. This does not prove either perturbation harmless. Irregular dark intervals average five time units and have no upper bound; successful captures there do not identify memory or prediction.

The experiment suggests prioritizing retention in the search strategy before extending this lineage into harder variants. Native held-out reports observe earlier-task performance but do not influence reproduction.
