# Postrun recommendation

Run one bounded whole-population diagnostic next; do not request more compute or immediately deepen either
search. This matched pair shows that the selection objective changes which capabilities survive, but it does
not yet show cumulative improvement in one endpoint over its inherited start. The existing local resources
are sufficient to resolve the more immediate question: did the populations lose joint candidates, or did a
noisy final endpoint rule fail to choose them?

All differences below are paired means ± SE over 512 shared lives. The fixed primary comparison,
`pareto4 − mean4`, favors Pareto for patch captures (+1.193 ± 0.334), replenished food (+1.162 ± 0.300),
POOL captures (+0.756 ± 0.213), and reserved-transfer captures (+1.475 ± 0.252). Transfer replenished food
increases by 1.658 ± 0.242. These are acquisition differences, not merely savings: Pareto spends an additional
223.606 ± 12.424 energy on patch lives and 206.177 ± 9.621 on transfer. The resulting F differences are
+0.364 ± 0.162 and +0.519 ± 0.119. Empty clicks also increase, by 2.545 ± 0.328 and 4.527 ± 0.393.

The cost is reduced pursuit: Pareto captures 0.709 ± 0.141 fewer persistent targets and 0.539 ± 0.114 fewer
long-gap targets than mean selection. Relative to its own `patch8_start`, Pareto recovers POOL captures
(+0.895 ± 0.208), but loses persistent (−0.674 ± 0.129) and long-gap (−0.479 ± 0.115) captures. Its own-start
patch gain is unresolved (+0.191 ± 0.302 captures; +0.232 ± 0.278 refill), while patch F falls
0.162 ± 0.147 with higher physical cost. Transfer refill improves (+0.781 ± 0.233); total transfer acquisition
is +0.682 ± 0.236, whose descriptive three-SE interval still crosses zero. Beating mean selection partly
reflects mean's patch decline (−1.002 ± 0.334), not established acquisition beyond the inherited endpoint.

Against AO, Pareto has more patch refill (+1.033 ± 0.299) and transfer refill (+1.064 ± 0.220), but loses
POOL (−1.320 ± 0.210), persistent (−0.795 ± 0.140), and long-gap (−0.428 ± 0.116) captures. Its descriptive
retention ratios are 91.65%, 86.47%, and 89.35%; all three registered 90% margin intervals cross zero.
Thus the losses are real endpoint tradeoffs, while none establishes a resolved crossing of that diagnostic
90% boundary. The same unchanged Pareto genome produced all five results. Refill provenance does not
establish stock estimation, revisits, or neural memory.

The agreed next diagnostic will score **every ordered row** of the saved G8, mean4 and pareto4 populations
on one fresh shared 32-seed development panel in the same five worlds: 96 × 32 × 5 = 15,360 calls.
Include unchanged AO rank zero on the same panel for paired ancestral comparisons (+160): **15,520 research
calls plus four startup calls, 15,524 total**. Keep prices, worlds, genomes and all current reporting endpoints fixed. Report every
row, the domain capture and F distributions, replenished food, the registered minimum relative-capture
statistic, and how each fixed endpoint sits within its population. Use the saved cached selection scores to
show ranking disagreement. No mutations, replacement endpoint, or newly declared winner belongs in this study.

This one panel tests a concrete bottleneck before another operator, exposure or cost intervention. Its small
sample cannot certify the best of 96 rows or prove joint candidates absent; apparent candidates would need
later prospective selection and fresh confirmation. Stop after the declared panel rather than drawing more
panels until one looks attractive. Population diversity alone is not cumulative capability in one organism.
Neither improvement over one ancestral endpoint nor this one matched evolutionary pair establishes novelty
relative to every founder, repeatability across searches, broad transfer, or a substrate/resource ceiling.
