# Preserving acquisition tradeoffs did not yet combine all abilities

In this matched pair, Pareto selection produced a fixed genome that gathered more food than ordinary mean-F
selection in trained patches, POOL and a reserved patch arrangement. It lost captures in persistent pursuit
and longer cue gaps. This is a useful change in which abilities survive, **not yet cumulative joint improvement**.
The same unchanged genome faces every world; no task-specific winner is assembled or substituted.

Both branches inherit all 32 ordered survivors of the preceding eight-generation resource continuation.
Each receives four generations, identical fixed worlds and physical prices, native mating/two point additions,
8 initial lives and 8 recheck lives. Mean selection ranks ordinary production fitness. Pareto selection preserves
tradeoffs among three per-world capture means and ordinary fitness. Both use the same cached-life endpoint rule:
maximize the weakest capture fraction relative to fixed AO means, then ordinary F, then native ID. This selects
mean rank 0 / ID 663 and Pareto rank 11 / ID 883 before confirmation. Native Pareto rank 0 is a separate diagnostic.

## Compare with the inherited start

Pareto's trained-patch acquisition versus G8 changes only **+0.191406 ± 0.302464**, unresolved. Its POOL recovery
is **+0.894531 ± 0.208193**, but persistent pursuit falls **0.673828 ± 0.128773** and longer-gap captures fall
**0.478516 ± 0.115094**. Transfer total capture gains 0.681641 ± 0.236301, short of three SE; replenished food gains
0.781250 ± 0.233359. Mean selection loses 1.001953 ± 0.333876 trained-patch captures versus G8, while its persistent
and longer-gap changes remain unresolved. Beating the other branch is therefore not equivalent to acquiring
a new patch ability beyond the starting endpoint.

Compared with the earlier AO reference, Pareto retains 91.65% of POOL captures, 86.47% of persistent captures
and 89.35% of longer-gap captures. All three 90% margins cross zero at ± 3 SE: neither joint 90% retention nor a
loss exceeding 10% is established. Absolute losses on those three old worlds are resolved. Mean has positive
lower 3 SE margins for the persistent and longer-gap 90% diagnostics, but leaves POOL unresolved at 86.87% point
retention. These diagnostics permit some loss and were not selection gates.
[All absolute values, six contrasts and cost components](TABLES.md).

One matched evolutionary pair cannot establish the reliability of a selection strategy. Life-level SE measures
uncertainty for these fixed genomes, not variation across searches. A gain over an ancestral rank-zero genome
also does not prove that it was absent from every member of the inherited 32-genome population. No new memory,
conditional resource-allocation algorithm, formal information-payoff admission or superiority to handwritten
algorithms is demonstrated.

## Next decision

A proposed next step is to inspect **every survivor** before choosing another search-policy change or extension.
One fixed small development panel for all 32 G8, mean4 and Pareto4 genomes across the five existing worlds can
distinguish useful population variation from noisy endpoint selection. The endpoint rule used only four
persistent-pursuit lives among its sixteen cached selection lives. This makes selection noise a concrete
possibility, not an established explanation for the losses.

Include the unchanged AO reference, retain all rows, and keep the present endpoints and conclusions fixed.
This diagnostic cannot certify a new winner selected from its own data. Any later prospectively chosen endpoint
needs fresh confirmation. If useful joint variants survive, a longer continuation becomes more attractive;
if the population itself trades away prior skills, reconsider how changes preserve useful combinations.
Local resources remain adequate. See [independent recommendation](POSTRUN_RECOMMENDATION.md).
