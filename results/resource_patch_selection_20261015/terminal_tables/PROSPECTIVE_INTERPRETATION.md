# Prospective interpretation checklist

Written before reading runtime outcomes. This organizes the registered comparisons; it adds no selection
rule, pass threshold or evaluation and does not choose a next protocol.

1. **Start with the fixed comparison.** Read `pareto4 − mean4` first, then each against `patch8_start` and
   `AO_start`. Both reporting endpoints use the same cached-life selection rule. Keep the native rank-zero
   diagnostics separate; neither native rank zero nor another survivor can replace a confirmed endpoint.
2. **Look for acquired food collection.** Separate total harvest into initial and replenished food, and
   separate its F contribution from savings, guard charges and extinction. Higher F alone is useful economy,
   not additional acquisition. Refill origin does not prove revisits, stock estimation or memory.
   Losing less than mean selection is not acquisition over the inherited start. Improvement over one rank-zero
   ancestor also does not show that the ability was absent from every member of the 32-genome founding population.
3. **Ask whether one organism retains and combines capability.** The same unchanged genome must support any
   claim across patch, POOL, persistent pursuit, longer cue gaps and reserved transfer. A Pareto population of
   different specialists is useful variation, but is not that result. State old-task capture losses directly;
   the registered 90% margins remain diagnostics, with unresolved margins distinguished from demonstrated loss.
4. **Show what the added acquisition costs.** Include physical cost components, F, empty clicks and missed
   attempts. A capture gain accompanied by lower F is an explicit tradeoff under unchanged prices. Larger
   bodies, extra activity or fewer empty clicks alone do not establish a more capable algorithm.
5. **Keep uncertainty at the right level.** Paired life SE and descriptive 3-SE bounds apply to these fixed
   endpoints. There is only one matched evolutionary pair, and the final common endpoint rule is noisy.
   Transfer concerns one new physical combination; it does not establish broad generality.

Extending the Pareto strategy becomes most attractive if its fixed endpoint adds patch/replenished food
relative to mean selection while preserving useful older abilities in that same organism, with transfer
supporting reuse and costs making the tradeoff intelligible. Perfect retention and a full circuit explanation
are not prerequisites. An extension should keep this direction simple rather than immediately tune many knobs.

If Pareto mainly preserves specialists, sacrifices older acquisition, or pays substantially more without
additional food, first use the already saved selection records to distinguish population diversity from joint
endpoint performance before choosing another selection direction. If both arms improve similarly, deeper ordinary
continuation may be the simpler investment. If neither establishes acquisition, this short pair does not establish
a substrate ceiling; select a focused next hypothesis rather than automatically deepen or begin a cost sweep.
These are qualitative priorities, not retrospective admission criteria.
