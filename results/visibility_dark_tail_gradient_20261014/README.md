# AG: retained-panel accessibility in fully dark flash

This is an unregistered source-only candidate. AF remains the active experiment. No inputs, seed IDs, genotypes, kernels or lives have been created for AG.

The question is whether ordinary mutations already present in the complete Y panels can improve fresh captures in AD's approved dark-flash world. Both backgrounds retain all 65 rows: the original parent and 64 recorded mutants. Selection uses unchanged production fitness, and both selected mutants remain in confirmation regardless of their screening gain. This tests a local fitness gradient, not the reliability of an evolutionary search or a memory mechanism.

The world is exactly AD's production ABI4 world at T = 1024: the existing `catch_speck_flash5_dim25_l4` preset with only `visibility_low = 0`. Target speed, turn persistence, delays, local rules, receptor background, capture value and physical prices are unchanged. AD measured useful information payoff; no body must pass a hand-built success gate. Its external controllers are not imported into organisms or this experiment.

Each background receives 32 shared screening seeds across all 65 rows. The highest mean production-F mutant among rows 1 through 64 wins, with the lowest row index breaking ties. Both winners are frozen before the confirmation cost gate. Four explicit roles, `P_D90`, `P_L92`, `H_D90`, `H_L92`, then receive the same 512 fresh seeds. Research costs 4,160 screening lives plus 2,048 confirmation lives. Startup adds one standard two-life ABI3 numerical-identity pair and eight fixed ABI4 calibration lives (parent and row 1, two seeds, both backgrounds): 6,218 calls in total. No new mutant generation is needed.

Fresh selected-mutant-minus-parent captures are the leading behavioral readout, accompanied by native `low_light_captures`, bright captures, unchanged production F, and the full capture-income, ordinary-cost, guard and extinction decomposition. A fitness gain supported only by lower costs is an economy result. Dark captures can include coasting or delayed actions; they do not establish memory or prediction. Both signs and uncertainties must be reported, including guarded and naturally extinct lives. Historical dim-flash outcomes are not fresh paired evidence about darkness.

One worker uses two simulation threads. The first-attempt monitor caps aggregate CPU at 5,400 seconds, startup plus screening at 3,400, and startup/calibration at 180; it preserves at least 2,000 for confirmation and stops new work 10 seconds before each relevant cap. All 130 actual AC dim-flash timing rows provide individual historical floors. Current fixed calibration raises those per-row screening floors; the pre-screen reserve prices each own parent and the most expensive potential mutant. After both winners are frozen, actual selected-row timings refine the reserve check. The 1.5 factor and 180-second confirmation allowance remain fixed. Estimates do not bound unseen dark-world cost, so a failed gate or hard cap leaves an incomplete study with no retry, substitution, smaller matrix or extension.

Historical timing floors alone, with a 180-second startup allowance, project 1,293.700 CPU seconds for screening and 1,206.546 for confirmation, below the 3,390 and 1,990 gates.
