The frozen AH records can establish whether the reserve changed selection and which recorded descendants followed. They cannot establish that a protected ancestor contributed a useful computation. This note uses source only; no AH runtime or outcome files were opened.

Each of the six protected slots is auditable against all 224 fresh children, the score-independent lottery, and the exact tie stream. A protected child can already rank highly enough to survive normally. Eligibility and protection therefore do not, by themselves, show a rescue.

The full 256-member initial rank identifies which protected children would miss the ordinary top-64 shortlist and exactly which ordinary shortlist members were displaced. The 64 measured pooled scores identify protection outside the ordinary top 32 **within the treatment shortlist**. That second comparison generally is not the complete ordinary survivor counterfactual: candidates displaced from the shortlist lack their four added lives in that arm. The reader deliberately labels these two quantities separately. When the treatment shortlist equals the ordinary shortlist, its complete ordinary survivor ranking is available directly.

Generation 0 has additional evidence: the control and reserve arms share all 256 candidate bytes, base scores, life IDs, and initial tie state. After the matched-cohort audit passes, the control's recorded ordinary shortlist and survivors provide an actual comparison for that same cohort; its rechecks also cover the ordinary candidates omitted by treatment. Later generations may contain different genotypes even when numeric child IDs coincide. Their control records cannot be substituted as counterfactual scores for the reserve trajectory.

All children retain both parent IDs, the crossover mask, point-mutation operations, and survival status. These support counts of subsequent parental use, genealogical descendants, continued survival, and membership in the final population or endpoint. The masks and recorded-operation replay distinguish a named parent from the genetic units actually transmitted. The existing `breeds` field follows a founder/parent-a chain, and the slot registry tracks gene-slot origins; neither is a marker of a protected child or its point mutations. Parentage alone can overstate transmission, and transmitted material can be neutral or overwritten. Neither establishes functional contribution or a necessary stepping stone.

The fresh endpoint comparison measures the realized effect of this combined change to evaluation allocation and breeder retention for one chosen start and one paired search seed. It does not isolate either component, identify memory, or establish policy reliability. A descendant of a rescued child plus better captures would be an interesting association with a precisely recorded route, not proof that the rescued change caused those captures. Better F through economy alone remains economy.

The next step should depend on the completed paired result. A capture-supported reserve-versus-control advantage warrants one independently seeded repetition of the same matched pair before claiming the policy is useful. Keep the same deliberately chosen AE L92 start, policy, prices, life counts, and fresh confirmation roles. Do not tune the reserve from this first trajectory. A positive comparison with control can coexist with an unresolved comparison with the start.

If there is no capture benefit, first summarize selection changes and descendant survival without new lives. A bounded longer ordinary search is then more directly useful than another small selection tweak: AF's continuing contraction leaves open whether acquisition returns after the easy economy gains. The full-population source design is in `ordinary_depth_source_design.md`. A small fresh evaluation of all six protected genotypes could describe the lottery's samples, but it is secondary to the depth question and cannot establish their causal contribution. An operationally failed or incompletely audited AH supports no scientific policy inference.
