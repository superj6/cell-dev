# Independent review of a fresh-offspring reserve

Read-only source review, 2026-10-14. No AG runtime outcomes, new seeds, genotypes, builds or lives were used. The proposal is `exploratory_reserve_candidate.md`: one matched four-generation pair from the fixed AE L92 start, with two uniformly chosen fresh offspring protected in generations 0–2 and ordinary selection in generation 3. F, prices, world, mutation and organism rules stay unchanged.

I recommend this as a small exploratory comparison, conditional on the concrete implementation checks below. It tests a different search mechanism from another depth extension. Its useful question is whether briefly permitting uncompetitive offspring to breed changes acquisition at the final endpoint. It does not establish that a necessary stepping stone was previously lost, and six protected offspring in one evolutionary pair give little evidence about policy reliability. Capture is a reported endpoint diagnostic; it must not become reserve eligibility or a new breeding reward.

A study-local recheck adapter is sufficient for the scientific change. `src/evolve.py:370` already returns the selected indices, pooled evaluations, shortlist, seeds and recheck results required by the unchanged evolution loop. Select two distinct offspring positions from the final 224 rows, then the top 62 nonreserved candidates for the 64-member recheck; retain those two plus the top 30 remaining pooled-F candidates. This changes both recheck allocation and survivor selection, which must be named as the intervention. No kernel, production ABI, cell input, world or new engine interface is required. Some small audit/identity glue is necessary; changing just the returned 32 indices would be insufficient.

Concrete pitfalls:

- **Ordering and ties.** The native loop calls `rank_desc` once before recheck; recheck calls it once over all 256 base scores and once over 64 pooled scores. Every call consumes a `randperm`, even without ties (`src/evolve.py:359–390,571–575`). Preserve these calls and shapes. Form the forced shortlist by filtering the already computed full base ranking; form and order survivors by filtering the already computed full pooled ranking. Do not rank subsets again or append reserved rows unsorted. `pop[0]` is used for validation, final scoring and exports, so it must remain the highest-F selected genome. In generation 3 delegate to the unchanged ordinary rule. A reserve-disabled adapter should reproduce ordinary results and tie state exactly.
- **Reserve randomness.** Use a separately pinned, stateless generation-keyed draw or a separately checkpointed stream. Never consume mutation or tie RNG for the reserve. A generation-keyed draw is the smaller solution because fresh positions alone determine eligibility; there is no persistent age counter. Uniform breeding gives protected parents an opportunity, not a guarantee of offspring. The pair must share the actual initial cohort and generator state, and verify its first 224 children bytewise. Later identical seed labels do not imply matched genotypes or synchronized draw usage after selection diverges.
- **Resume identity.** `checkpoint.numerical_identity()` hashes `src/**/*.py` and the kernel, not a script under `outputs/`; a monkey-patched recheck can otherwise change silently across resume (`src/checkpoint.py:82–115`). Include the exact adapter digest and complete policy in the existing identity/config validation before loading a checkpoint. The identity wrapper must call the original identity exactly once per worker; each extra call adds two golden lives. The current snapshot already saves the mutation and tie states (`src/evolve.py:516–540`), but would not save a new mutable reserve stream automatically. Do not resume old ordinary checkpoints under the treatment policy.
- **Recorded replay.** The current offline audit explicitly requires top-32 pooled-F survivors and a top-F shortlist (`results/prediction_continuation_20261014/analysis/helpers/selection.py:287–331`); reusing it unchanged would reject this policy or conceal a disabled check. Add a narrow policy-aware verification of protected indices, shortlist, pooled order, survivors and final ordinary generation. Existing lineage logs contain all child base scores but not the fresh base scores of every excluded parent. Retain all 256 candidate IDs/base scores, all 64 pooled/recheck scores, complete relevant ranks and reserve indices so the top-62 step is independently checkable. Prefer including this metadata in the existing lineage log: checkpoint restore currently verifies/truncates only curves, lineage and clones, so a separate sidecar needs its own crash-consistent offsets.
- **Endpoints and cost.** Freeze both generation-3 rank-zero endpoints before fresh paired confirmation; retain duplicate roles. Equal 64-member rechecks preserve the declared life count, but uniform forced offspring can be much more expensive than the old winners. Historical timing floors are assumptions, not caps. Hard phase and total limits remain essential; a stopped run is an incomplete feasibility outcome, not evidence against the reserve's scientific effect. Do not rescue a costly draw with a replacement or extra retry. Health failures must follow the existing protocol rather than silently bypassing protection.

Before a run, the focused evidence needed is modest: synthetic forced-shortlist/survivor and tie cases, ordinary-mode equivalence, policy-aware recorded replay, and one bounded uninterrupted-versus-resumed check with exact state/log agreement. The latter is an engineering experiment requiring its own declared calls and resource accounting; it is not authorized or performed by this source-only note. Existing monitor lifecycle, ledger accounting and production compatibility need no broad rewrite.

I found no user-approval blocker for a genuinely small two-arm pilot within a root-approved fixed local budget. The handoff explicitly preapproves minor “selection details” (`docs/HANDOFF.md:448–457`), and says the external search loop may use ML machinery. A generation index used only outside the organism is not an in-life clock. The restrictions on prices, stated goals, hand-designed organism behavior, ABI changes, paid compute and population-responsive worlds still apply. A capture/F Pareto rule or large selection matrix would be a different decision. Current authorization covers this assessment only; root must still choose the pilot, freeze its complete contract and provenance, and release it separately.
