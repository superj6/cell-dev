# AH complete: the reserve endpoint captured more than its matched control

In this one matched four-generation pair, reserving two uniformly chosen fresh
offspring during the first three selection rounds produced an endpoint with
**0.556641 ± 0.110184 more captures per life** than ordinary selection, on 512
fresh shared seeds. Its production fitness F was **0.254917 ± 0.054269 higher**.
Both differences exceed the registered strict three-standard-error threshold.
All uncertainties here are paired standard errors for these fixed genotypes.

The relative F advantage is supported by capture income: +0.271797 F, alongside
0.016880 F higher mean ordinary costs. The cost difference itself does not
exceed three standard errors. Mean captures were 3.306641 for the control and
3.863281 for the reserve endpoint; the shared starting genotype averaged
3.525391.

The comparison against the shared start is weaker for acquisition. The reserve
endpoint gained 0.337891 ± 0.115967 captures per life, narrowly below three
standard errors; the control changed by −0.218750 ± 0.115289. Both improved F
against the start, mostly through lower costs: control +1.094887 F with
1.201699 F cost savings, reserve +1.349804 F with 1.184818 F cost savings.
The result therefore supports a relative endpoint advantage in this selected
pair, not a resolved acquisition gain over the start or a generally reliable
selection policy.
