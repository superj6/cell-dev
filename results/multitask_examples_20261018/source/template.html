What changed as evolution continued?

Watch three saved organisms from the same population at different generations. Each grows its own tissue, senses nearby signals and moves through the world. The panels share a task and starting seed.

Later examples use the same pursuit-score selection rule; the original is the first imported row. These are not a direct ancestor–descendant chain. One life illustrates behavior; it does not establish an average improvement or a circuit’s mechanism.

The original example is the first genome in the pre-flash population. It does not represent the best of all 96 starting genomes. Later examples have the highest training pursuit score and are then measured on separate progress panels. The final cross-task assessment is still pending.

Each card shows a saved simulation, with no new evolution or simulation in your browser. Colors mark positional tissue layers, not learned cell categories. Cells communicate only with neighbors. Their changing local rules and growth were evolved.

Task phases, patch stock and food origin are labels for us. They are not extra information available to cells. Matching outcomes count correct and wrong choices, rather than treating either physical target as always correct.

The charts elsewhere use progress-panel lives. These replays use separate fixed illustration seeds, so their individual catch counts need not equal the chart averages.
