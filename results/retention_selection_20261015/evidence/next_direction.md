# What to try after AL

This is an offline recommendation written while AL runs, before reading its outcomes. No new study, seed, genome, build or simulation is prepared here. Keep the same local cell substrate, ordinary genetics, physical prices, two-thread execution and explicit CPU/memory stops.

The useful target is cumulative acquisition: recover captures in POOL while preserving the persistent-target gain, then transfer that combination to nearby conditions. Higher net fitness alone is insufficient. AK measured a loss of 7.328 ± 0.352 POOL captures from original L92 to AI-control, despite a persistent gain of 0.680 ± 0.176 over AE-L92. That is a concrete capability to recover. AK's nearby transfer was real, but its robustness interactions were unresolved. See [AK results](../../RESULTS.md#2026-10-15--ak-complete-nearby-transfer-but-substantial-ancestral-capability-loss) and [raw summary](../../results/retention_frontier_20261015/analysis/summary.json).

| AL result | Most useful next bounded experiment | What it would answer |
|---|---|---|
| Mixture recovers POOL captures over its start and retains persistent captures | Repeat the same matched ordinary-versus-mixture pair once with independent search and confirmation seeds, from the same full 32-row AI population. Keep AL's 4+4 lives, four generations and confirmation matrix. | Whether the encouraging combination recurs in this chosen lineage. AH's reserve advantage reversed in AI, so a single positive pair is a weak basis for adopting a selection change. Count the repeat as a search replicate; do not pool confirmation lives as evolutionary replicates. |
| Mixture improves one training context but loses the other, or endpoint differences remain unresolved | One matched-budget precision-versus-depth comparison **within mixture selection**, from the same full AI population: four generations at 4+4 versus two at 8+8, with shared first-cohort genomes and a predetermined seed-overlap rule. Retain two endpoints plus start on the same four-world confirmation design. | Whether spending the same selection calls on better estimates instead of more generations changes the tradeoff. This compares allocations, not precision in isolation, and one pair cannot establish reliability. A clearly resolved tradeoff can also reflect the fixed objective or reachable mutations; noise is only a hypothesis. |
| Both branches mainly save costs, without supported own-start capture recovery | One eight-generation ordinary mixture continuation from **all 32 final mixture survivors**, with new RNG/scores, fixed world allocation and unchanged prices. Confirm the final endpoint, the AL mixture start and AI start on fresh paired lives in all four contexts. | Whether acquisition starts to improve after the cheaper phenotype has been selected, without resetting through a rank-zero founder bottleneck. If it only buys further economy, stop extending this lineage by default rather than label that algorithmic progress. |

The first two options use the same 10,240 selection calls as AL. The eight-generation single branch also uses 10,240 selection calls; with validation at generations 0/4/7, six tiny final calls, 3,072 confirmation lives and six startup lives, it would total **13,372 calls**. Its cost must be re-estimated from completed AL timings before release. These are alternatives, not an authorized three-study matrix. None currently needs paid compute or a larger rollout. AL's conservative pre-run projection was about 4,460 CPU-seconds under its 5,400 cap, with only about 251 seconds below the search stop threshold; actual bounded execution will be the better planning anchor.

Four generations are a short search horizon. Four initial plus four recheck lives are also sparse: mixture provides only two lives per context at the initial gate and four per context after recheck. Yet sparse evaluation alone does not establish a ranking problem. Shared seeds, covariance between candidates, adaptive shortlisting, different genotypes and fitness/cost covariance prevent converting a final paired capture SE into native selection error. AB's earlier precision-versus-depth comparison did not show a net-fitness advantage, and its relative D90 capture benefit came from less deterioration, not a confirmed own-start capture gain ([AB results](../../RESULTS.md#2026-10-14--more-precise-scoring-does-not-show-a-fitness-advantage-over-depth)). Use AL's recorded initial/recheck ranking changes descriptively before attributing failure to noise; unsaved per-life search ledgers cannot support a full ranking-error reconstruction.

For all branches, report raw captures, own-start changes, the existing 90% retention margin and actual simultaneous gains separately. A positive retention margin permits some loss. Keep capture income and ordinary cost savings separate, retain every fixed role and context, and treat AL's revealed combined turn/blink case as development data thereafter. Additional fixed challenge cases should be chosen prospectively once a retained improvement exists. Even a successful transfer establishes useful behavior across these environments; it does not identify memory, prediction or a sophisticated internal algorithm without subsequent behavioral evidence.
